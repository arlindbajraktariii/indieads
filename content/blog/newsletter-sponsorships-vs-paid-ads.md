When an indie founder decides to pay for attention, two options dominate the conversation: sponsor a newsletter, or run ads. Both are legitimate. They fail for different reasons, and they demand different things from you.

Here is how to choose without wasting a month.

## What newsletter sponsorships are good at

A sponsorship borrows trust. The reader already has a relationship with the writer, and your placement arrives inside that relationship rather than next to it.

Strengths:

- **Relevance.** Niche newsletters have a narrow, self-selected audience.
- **Attention.** Readers open the email; they do not scroll past it in a feed.
- **Credibility.** A thoughtful mention reads as a recommendation.
- **Simplicity.** No campaign builder, no auction, no algorithm.

Weaknesses:

- **Limited inventory.** You cannot scale a single slot indefinitely.
- **Fuzzy attribution.** Opens and clicks are estimates, and conversions depend on your landing page.
- **Quality varies.** A big list with low engagement is worse than a small list with high engagement.

Ask for the open rate, the click rate and a recent example of a sponsor that performed. If the publisher does not track clicks, treat the placement as brand awareness, not acquisition.

## What paid ads are good at

Ads are a machine for testing messages. You can change the copy, the audience and the budget without negotiating with a human.

Strengths:

- **Fast iteration.** Launch today, learn this week.
- **Control.** You decide when to pause, increase or rewrite.
- **Measurability.** Impressions, clicks and conversions are attributable (within the usual caveats).
- **Scale.** Once a campaign works, you can add budget.

Weaknesses:

- **No borrowed trust.** You are an interruption.
- **Learning costs.** Small budgets produce noisy data.
- **Creative fatigue.** The same ad stops working.
- **Platform minimums.** The big networks are built for larger spenders.

## The real cost comparison

Do not compare the sticker price of a sponsorship with the cost per click of an ad. Compare cost per *qualified* visit.

A €400 sponsorship that sends 120 readers who understand your product can be cheaper than €400 of clicks that send 400 people who bounce.

To compare fairly, you need:

1. A unique link or code per placement.
2. A landing page that converts cold traffic.
3. A definition of “qualified” — signed up, started a trial, booked a call.

Without those three, every channel looks the same and you will choose based on vibes.

## Tracking both without lying to yourself

- **Use one link per placement.** UTMs are fine, unique short links are better.
- **Watch the second visit.** Many readers come back later; last-click attribution will miss them.
- **Ask at signup.** “How did you hear about us?” catches what analytics loses.
- **Give it two weeks.** Early numbers are noisy in both directions.

## When to use which

**Choose a newsletter sponsorship when:**

- Your audience is concentrated in a few publications.
- You want credibility more than volume.
- You have a clear offer and a page that converts.
- You can afford to treat the first placement as a test.

**Choose paid ads when:**

- You need to test several messages quickly.
- Your audience is broad enough to target contextually.
- You have a funnel that already converts organic traffic.
- You want to control spend week by week.

## Run both, but not at once

The mistake is launching both in the same week. You will not know which one worked, and you will spend twice as much attention.

Run one for two weeks. Write down what happened. Then run the other with a hypothesis borrowed from the first.

Both channels are tools. Neither is a strategy. The strategy is knowing which question you are trying to answer this month.
