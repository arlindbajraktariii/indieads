A large ad network can survive a bad advertiser. A small one cannot. When the audience is a few thousand indie builders and the publishers are people you know by name, every questionable ad costs more than the impression is worth.

This is not a moral lecture. It is an operating constraint. Here are the rules we apply, and why.

## No fake urgency

Countdown timers that reset, “only 3 spots left” on a product with unlimited seats, prices that are always about to increase. These work in the short term and rot the audience in the long term.

If there is a real deadline, say what it is and why it exists. If there is not, do not invent one.

## Disclose sponsored placement

Readers deserve to know when they are looking at an ad. That does not make the ad less effective; it makes the publisher trustworthy.

For newsletter and native placements, the label should be visible without hovering, and it should say “sponsored” or “ad” — not a vague icon that requires interpretation.

## Respect the publisher’s audience

A publisher decides what appears next to their work. That means:

- No ads that break the page layout.
- No auto-playing sound or video.
- No pop-ups that hijack navigation.
- No categories the publisher has explicitly excluded.

Good networks make this easy to enforce. Bad ones make publishers choose between revenue and their own site.

## Be honest about the product

Ads that promise outcomes the product cannot deliver create refunds, churn and angry threads. At small scale, those threads are your reputation.

We reject campaigns that:

- Claim guaranteed results.
- Use fake testimonials or invented logos.
- Hide the price until a sales call.
- Pretend to be editorial content.

The bar is not “legally defensible”. It is “would we be comfortable if the reader was a friend”.

## Take privacy seriously

Indie audiences are unusually aware of tracking. Respect that:

- Collect the minimum data needed to report on a campaign.
- Do not sell or share personal data.
- Explain what is collected in a plain-language privacy policy.
- Make it easy to ask for deletion.

Our [privacy policy](/privacy) is written to be read, not skimmed by a lawyer. If something in it is unclear, that is a bug.

## Reject the categories that erode trust

Some ad categories reliably damage the surrounding content: get-rich-quick schemes, crypto pumps, fake software cracks, predatory loans. The short-term revenue is not worth the long-term audience.

Publishers should be able to exclude categories, and advertisers should know the rules before they submit. Our [advertising guidelines](/ad-guidelines) list what we accept and what we do not.

## What this means for advertisers

Fewer places to cut corners, but a better environment for honest products. When the audience trusts the placement, a relevant ad performs better than a trick ever could.

## What this means for publishers

You should never have to audit every ad manually. A network that enforces its own rules is worth more than one that pays slightly more per impression.
