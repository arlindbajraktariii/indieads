The first hundred users are a research project disguised as a growth problem. You are not trying to reach everyone who might want the product. You are trying to find the specific people who already feel the pain, and learn from them fast enough to build the next version.

That is good news for a solo founder. The work is manual, but it is cheap, and it compounds.

## Talk to twenty people before you optimize a funnel

Before a single campaign, have twenty conversations with people in your target audience. Not pitches. Questions:

- How do you handle this today?
- What is annoying about that?
- Have you tried a tool for it? Why did you stop?

You will hear the same three complaints in different words. Those complaints become your headline, your onboarding, and your ad copy. This is the highest-leverage work you can do, and it costs nothing but time.

## Go where the problem is discussed

The best channel is not the biggest one. It is the one where people describe the problem in their own words. Look for:

- Forum threads and Reddit communities around the workflow.
- GitHub issues on adjacent open-source projects.
- Slack and Discord groups for the tools your users already use.
- Newsletters that cover the niche, not just the industry.

Participate as a person, not a brand. Answer questions. Link your product only when it genuinely solves the thing being asked.

## Do things that do not scale

There is a phase where manual work beats automation. Use it:

- Onboard your first ten users on a call.
- Set up their accounts yourself.
- Ask for feedback the day after they use it.
- Fix the top complaint before you write another line of marketing copy.

This feels slow. It is actually the fastest route to product-market fit, because you are compressing months of guessing into weeks of conversations.

## Make sharing easy, not mandatory

Word of mouth happens when the product is useful and visible. You cannot force it, but you can remove friction:

- A shareable result or report.
- A public page users are proud to send to a colleague.
- A referral that gives both sides something real.
- An invite flow that does not require a call.

Avoid “share to unlock” mechanics. They buy you a spike and cost you trust.

## Track where every signup came from

You do not need a data warehouse. You need one extra field at signup and a habit:

- Add a hidden field for the source.
- Use different links for each channel.
- Ask new users “how did you hear about us?” in onboarding.

After fifty signups, patterns appear. Double down on the channel that produced the users who stayed, not the one with the biggest numbers.

## Add paid distribution only after the loop works

Paid ads amplify a working funnel. They do not fix a broken one. A useful rule: if you cannot get ten users from manual outreach, a campaign will not get you a hundred.

Once you have:

1. a landing page that converts cold traffic,
2. a product that retains the people who sign up,
3. a clear idea of what a user is worth,

then paid channels make sense. That is the point where a small test budget produces information instead of noise.

## The unglamorous summary

- Twenty conversations beat twenty thousand impressions.
- One channel done properly beats five done occasionally.
- Retention is the only proof that the first hundred users were real.
