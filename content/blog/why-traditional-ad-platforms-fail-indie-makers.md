You shipped the product. You posted it on X, Reddit and a few directories. A handful of people clicked, two signed up, and then the graph went flat. So you open Google Ads, start a campaign, and immediately run into a wall of settings, minimum budgets and a “learning phase” that sounds suspiciously like “spend money and hope”.

That experience is not a personal failure. It is the result of using a platform designed for a completely different customer: a company with a marketing team, a five-figure monthly budget, and enough traffic to make statistical optimization possible.

## The economics are built for someone else

Large ad platforms optimize for total spend. Their auction rewards campaigns that can absorb a learning period, test dozens of creatives, and wait weeks for conversion data to stabilize.

For an indie product, the math is inverted:

- You need feedback in days, not months.
- A €300 test is a real decision, not a rounding error.
- You have one landing page and one offer, not a portfolio.

When you cannot feed the algorithm enough data, it does what algorithms do: guesses. And you pay for the guesswork.

## The targeting is guesswork at small scale

Interest targeting, lookalike audiences and “similar to converters” all depend on having converters in the first place. At the beginning you do not. So you end up targeting broad categories like “technology” or “small business owners”, which is another way of saying “everyone”.

Contextual targeting works differently. Instead of asking a platform to infer who someone is, you place your message next to content they are already reading. A developer reading about CI pipelines is a better signal than a checkbox that says “software”.

That is the core idea behind RoketAd: reach people inside the independent tech ecosystem, where the context does most of the targeting for you.

## The creative process assumes a team

Enterprise ad accounts run on volume: ten hooks, five images, three audiences, continuous iteration. That workflow assumes a designer, a copywriter and someone to read the dashboard.

Indie makers have an hour on a Tuesday night. What works at that scale is the opposite: one clear offer, one honest visual, and a small number of deliberate tests.

## The audience is not where the big platforms think it is

Developers and technical founders are famously hard to reach with interruptive advertising. Many use ad blockers. Many ignore display banners by reflex.

What they do respond to is relevance: a tool that solves the problem in the article they are reading, or a product recommended by a site they already trust. That is closer to a sponsorship than to a traditional display campaign.

## What small budgets actually need

If you are spending less than a few thousand per month, prioritize:

1. **Context over identity.** Place ads where your audience already is, not where a platform thinks they might be.
2. **A short feedback loop.** See impressions, clicks and signups within a week.
3. **Simple pricing.** No hidden fees, no minimum spend that eats the experiment.
4. **One number to watch.** Cost per signup, not a dashboard of twenty metrics.
5. **Room to be wrong.** A test budget you can lose without losing sleep.
